# 02 — Taxonomy and Tags

Multi-dimensional categorization of all 24 thinking systems across 12 tag dimensions.

---

## Tag Dimensions

| # | Dimension | Description |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Nature** | Descriptive (how thinking works) vs. Prescriptive (how to think better) vs. Meta-framework |
| 2 | **Thinking Phase** | Perceiving, Framing, Generating, Evaluating, Deciding, Reflecting |
| 3 | **Locus of Cognition** | Individual, Dialogical, Team, Environmental, Artifactual |
| 4 | **Problem Type** | Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic, Novel, Competitive, All |
| 5 | **Temporal Orientation** | Backward-looking, Present-focused, Forward-looking |
| 6 | **Cognitive Mode** | Analytical, Creative, Intuitive, Integrative, Narrative, Somatic, Reflective, Adaptive |
| 7 | **Domain of Origin** | Philosophy, Psychology, Military, Economics, Design, Biology, Engineering, etc. |
| 8 | **Scale** | Personal, Team, Organizational, Societal |
| 9 | **Cognitive Load** | Low, Moderate, High, Very High |
| 10 | **Output Type** | Understanding, Judgment, Ideas, Decision, Model, Solution, Calibrated Confidence, etc. |
| 11 | **Epistemic Stance** | Empirical, Skeptical, Constructivist, Probabilistic, Pragmatic, Foundationalist, etc. |
| 12 | **Learnability** | Conceptual, Skill-based, Technique-based, Process-based, Developmental, Lifelong |

---

## Full Tagging Matrix

### 1. Dual-Process Theory (System 1 & 2)

- **Nature:** Descriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Evaluating
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Intuitive
- **Domain:** Psychology, Behavioral Economics
- **Scale:** Personal
- **Cognitive Load:** Low (describes what already happens)
- **Output:** Understanding
- **Epistemic Stance:** Empirical
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — you learn to notice, not to execute
- **Complements:** Metacognition, Bayesian Thinking, Bounded Rationality
- **Anti-patterns:** Assuming System 2 is always better; believing awareness of bias eliminates it

### 2. Critical Thinking

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Reflecting
- **Locus:** Individual, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Clear, Complicated
- **Temporal:** Present, Backward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical
- **Domain:** Philosophy, Education
- **Scale:** Personal, Team
- **Cognitive Load:** High
- **Output:** Judgment, Understanding
- **Epistemic Stance:** Skeptical-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — improves with deliberate practice
- **Complements:** Socratic Method, Bayesian Thinking, Bloom's Taxonomy
- **Anti-patterns:** Weaponizing skepticism to dismiss inconvenient evidence; analysis paralysis

### 3. Design Thinking

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Framing, Generating, Evaluating, Deciding
- **Locus:** Team, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Complex, Novel
- **Temporal:** Present, Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Creative, Empathic, Analytical
- **Domain:** Design, Engineering, Business
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** High
- **Output:** Solution, Prototype, Reframed problem
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Experimental
- **Learnability:** Process-based — follow the steps, refine with experience
- **Complements:** Convergent/Divergent Thinking, Systems Thinking, Embodied Cognition
- **Anti-patterns:** Design theater (going through motions without real empathy); prototype fetishism

### 4. Systems Thinking

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Framing
- **Locus:** Individual, Team, Environmental
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Present, Forward, Backward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Holistic
- **Domain:** Biology, Engineering, Management Science
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational, Societal
- **Cognitive Load:** Very High
- **Output:** Understanding, Model, Leverage point
- **Epistemic Stance:** Holistic-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Conceptual + practice — requires sustained effort to internalize
- **Complements:** Theory of Constraints, Cynefin Framework, Second-Order Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** Analysis paralysis from seeing too many connections; unfalsifiable circular explanations

### 5. Lateral Thinking & Six Thinking Hats

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Generating, Framing
- **Locus:** Individual, Team, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Complex, Novel
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Creative, Associative
- **Domain:** Psychology, Creativity Research
- **Scale:** Personal, Team
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Ideas, Reframed problem, New perspectives
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Constructive
- **Learnability:** Technique-based — learnable quickly, mastery takes practice
- **Complements:** Divergent Thinking, Design Thinking, First Principles
- **Anti-patterns:** Using randomness as excuse for lack of rigor; hat-wearing as performance

### 6. First Principles Thinking

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Framing, Generating
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** Novel, Complex
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Creative
- **Domain:** Philosophy, Physics, Engineering
- **Scale:** Personal, Team
- **Cognitive Load:** Very High
- **Output:** Foundational understanding, Novel solution
- **Epistemic Stance:** Foundationalist-Rational
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — easy to understand, very hard to execute well
- **Complements:** Inversion, Socratic Method, Lateral Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** False first principles (smuggled assumptions); reinventing the wheel when analogy would suffice

### 7. Dialectical Thinking

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Framing, Generating, Reflecting
- **Locus:** Individual, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Complex
- **Temporal:** Present, Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Integrative, Paradoxical
- **Domain:** Philosophy, Psychology, Political Theory
- **Scale:** Personal, Societal
- **Cognitive Load:** High
- **Output:** Synthesis, Reframed understanding, Integrated position
- **Epistemic Stance:** Constructivist-Processual
- **Learnability:** Developmental — requires cognitive maturity, hard to rush
- **Complements:** Socratic Method, Sensemaking, Systems Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** False synthesis (compromise without genuine integration); infinite regress

### 8. Deductive / Inductive / Abductive Reasoning

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Generating
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** Clear, Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Creative (abduction)
- **Domain:** Philosophy, Logic, Science
- **Scale:** Personal
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate to High
- **Output:** Inference, Hypothesis, Conclusion
- **Epistemic Stance:** Logical-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — can be taught formally
- **Complements:** Critical Thinking, Bayesian Thinking, Socratic Method
- **Anti-patterns:** Treating inductive conclusions as deductive certainties; confirmation bias in abduction

### 9. Metacognition

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Reflecting, All phases (monitoring layer)
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Present, Backward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Reflective, Executive
- **Domain:** Psychology, Education
- **Scale:** Personal
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate (becomes automatic with practice)
- **Output:** Self-awareness, Strategy adjustment, Calibrated confidence
- **Epistemic Stance:** Reflective-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — highly teachable with large transfer effects
- **Complements:** Dual-Process Theory, Bayesian Thinking, Bloom's Taxonomy
- **Anti-patterns:** Metacognitive illusion (feeling like you understand when you don't); excessive self-monitoring that disrupts flow

### 10. Bayesian Thinking

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Reflecting
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Present, Backward (updating from new data)
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Probabilistic
- **Domain:** Mathematics, Statistics, Philosophy of Science
- **Scale:** Personal, Team
- **Cognitive Load:** High (formally), Moderate (as mindset)
- **Output:** Updated belief, Calibrated confidence, Decision under uncertainty
- **Epistemic Stance:** Probabilistic-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Conceptual + practice — intuition develops with calibration training
- **Complements:** Probabilistic Thinking, Critical Thinking, Metacognition
- **Anti-patterns:** Ignoring base rates; anchoring too heavily on priors; treating posterior as certainty

### 11. OODA Loop

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Framing, Deciding
- **Locus:** Individual, Team
- **Problem Type:** Chaotic, Complex, Competitive
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Adaptive, Intuitive, Analytical
- **Domain:** Military Strategy, Competitive Strategy
- **Scale:** Personal, Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate to High
- **Output:** Decision, Action, Tempo advantage
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Adaptive
- **Learnability:** Process-based — learnable, mastery requires live practice
- **Complements:** Cynefin Framework, Sensemaking, Munger's Latticework
- **Anti-patterns:** Fetishizing speed over orientation quality; treating it as linear when it's dynamic

### 12. Cynefin Framework

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Framing
- **Locus:** Team, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Meta — classifies all problem types
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Holistic, Adaptive
- **Domain:** Complexity Science, Knowledge Management, Organizational Theory
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational, Societal
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Classification, Appropriate method selection, Sensemaking
- **Epistemic Stance:** Complexity-Aware
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — easy to learn, hard to apply honestly (people miscategorize)
- **Complements:** OODA Loop, Systems Thinking, Sensemaking
- **Anti-patterns:** Categorizing everything as "complicated" to justify expert control; treating domains as fixed

### 13. Convergent & Divergent Thinking

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Generating (divergent), Evaluating (convergent)
- **Locus:** Individual, Team
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Creative (divergent), Analytical (convergent)
- **Domain:** Psychology, Creativity Research, Design
- **Scale:** Personal, Team
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Ideas (divergent), Decision (convergent)
- **Epistemic Stance:** Empirical-Pragmatic
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — divergent thinking is trainable
- **Complements:** Design Thinking, Lateral Thinking, Six Thinking Hats
- **Anti-patterns:** Premature convergence; infinite divergence; confusing brainstorm quantity with quality

### 14. Munger's Latticework of Mental Models

- **Nature:** Prescriptive, Meta-framework
- **Phase:** All phases — it's an operating system, not a single tool
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** All — especially complicated and complex
- **Temporal:** All
- **Cognitive Mode:** Integrative, Multidisciplinary
- **Domain:** Investing, Philosophy, Polymath tradition
- **Scale:** Personal
- **Cognitive Load:** Very High (building it), Moderate (using it once built)
- **Output:** Integrated understanding, Better decisions, Pattern recognition
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Pluralist
- **Learnability:** Lifelong project — never "done"
- **Complements:** All other models (it contains them), First Principles, Inversion
- **Anti-patterns:** Collecting models without practicing application; treating it as intellectual flex

### 15. Socratic Method

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Framing, Evaluating, Reflecting
- **Locus:** Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** All — especially beliefs and assumptions
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Reflective, Dialectical
- **Domain:** Philosophy
- **Scale:** Personal, Team (dialogue)
- **Cognitive Load:** High (for both questioner and respondent)
- **Output:** Clarified understanding, Exposed assumptions, Refined beliefs
- **Epistemic Stance:** Skeptical-Constructive
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — improves greatly with practice
- **Complements:** Critical Thinking, Dialectical Thinking, Metacognition
- **Anti-patterns:** Gotcha questioning disguised as Socratic inquiry; infinite regress without progress

### 16. Sensemaking

- **Nature:** Descriptive, Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Perceiving, Framing
- **Locus:** Individual, Team, Organizational
- **Problem Type:** Complex, Chaotic
- **Temporal:** Backward (retrospective), Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Narrative, Intuitive, Social
- **Domain:** Organizational Theory, Communication Studies, Naturalistic Decision Making
- **Scale:** Personal, Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Plausible narrative, Actionable understanding, Coherence
- **Epistemic Stance:** Constructivist-Pragmatic
- **Learnability:** Partly innate — can be enhanced through awareness and practice
- **Complements:** Cynefin Framework, OODA Loop, Metacognition
- **Anti-patterns:** Confusing a plausible narrative with the truth; sensemaking collapse under stress

### 17. Bounded Rationality & Satisficing

- **Nature:** Descriptive
- **Phase:** Deciding
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Adaptive, Pragmatic
- **Domain:** Economics, Cognitive Science, AI
- **Scale:** Personal, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Low (describes what already happens)
- **Output:** Understanding of decision limits, Permission to satisfice
- **Epistemic Stance:** Empirical-Pragmatic
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — changes mindset about what "good decisions" look like
- **Complements:** Dual-Process Theory, Metacognition, Probabilistic Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** Using satisficing as excuse for laziness on high-stakes decisions; over-optimizing trivial choices

### 18. Theory of Constraints

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Framing, Deciding
- **Locus:** Team, Organizational
- **Problem Type:** Complicated
- **Temporal:** Present, Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Systemic
- **Domain:** Manufacturing, Operations Management, Business
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Identified bottleneck, Focused improvement plan
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Process-based — straightforward to learn, discipline to maintain
- **Complements:** Systems Thinking, First Principles, Inversion
- **Anti-patterns:** Improving non-constraints; failing to re-identify after elevating

### 19. Inversion

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Framing, Evaluating
- **Locus:** Individual, Team
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Creative (perspective shift)
- **Domain:** Mathematics, Philosophy, Investing
- **Scale:** Personal, Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate
- **Output:** Risk identification, Failure avoidance, Reframed problem
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Skeptical
- **Learnability:** Technique-based — easy to learn and apply immediately
- **Complements:** Pre-mortem, First Principles, Second-Order Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** Excessive pessimism; only inverting without also reasoning forward

### 20. Probabilistic Thinking

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Deciding
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Probabilistic
- **Domain:** Mathematics, Statistics, Decision Science
- **Scale:** Personal, Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Moderate to High
- **Output:** Calibrated forecast, Expected value, Decision under uncertainty
- **Epistemic Stance:** Probabilistic-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Skill-based — calibration improves dramatically with practice
- **Complements:** Bayesian Thinking, Metacognition, Pre-mortem
- **Anti-patterns:** False precision; ignoring fat tails

### 21. Embodied Cognition

- **Nature:** Descriptive
- **Phase:** All — it's a theory of how all cognition works
- **Locus:** Individual, Environmental
- **Problem Type:** All
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Somatic, Sensorimotor, Spatial
- **Domain:** Philosophy, Cognitive Science, Neuroscience
- **Scale:** Personal
- **Cognitive Load:** Low (describes what already happens)
- **Output:** Understanding of cognition, Design implications
- **Epistemic Stance:** Phenomenological-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — awareness changes practice
- **Complements:** Distributed Cognition, Design Thinking, Metacognition
- **Anti-patterns:** Over-claiming (not all thought is bodily); ignoring abstract reasoning capacities

### 22. Distributed Cognition

- **Nature:** Descriptive
- **Phase:** All — theory of how group cognition works
- **Locus:** Team, Environmental, Artifactual
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Present
- **Cognitive Mode:** Collaborative, Systemic
- **Domain:** Cognitive Science, Anthropology, HCI
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Low (describes what already happens)
- **Output:** Understanding of group intelligence, Design principles for teams and tools
- **Epistemic Stance:** Ecological-Empirical
- **Learnability:** Conceptual — changes how you design teams and workflows
- **Complements:** Embodied Cognition, Systems Thinking, Sensemaking
- **Anti-patterns:** Ignoring individual cognition; assuming distribution automatically improves performance

### 23. Pre-mortem (Prospective Hindsight)

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Deciding
- **Locus:** Team, Dialogical
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Forward (via imagined backward)
- **Cognitive Mode:** Imaginative, Analytical, Social
- **Domain:** Psychology, Project Management, Military Planning
- **Scale:** Team, Organizational
- **Cognitive Load:** Low to Moderate
- **Output:** Risk identification, Failure prevention, Improved plans
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Skeptical
- **Learnability:** Technique-based — immediately usable
- **Complements:** Inversion, Second-Order Thinking, Probabilistic Thinking
- **Anti-patterns:** Doing it perfunctorily; identifying risks but failing to act on them

### 24. Second-Order Thinking

- **Nature:** Prescriptive
- **Phase:** Evaluating, Deciding
- **Locus:** Individual
- **Problem Type:** Complicated, Complex
- **Temporal:** Forward
- **Cognitive Mode:** Analytical, Systemic, Strategic
- **Domain:** Economics, Investing, Strategy
- **Scale:** Personal, Organizational, Societal
- **Cognitive Load:** High
- **Output:** Anticipated consequences, Better strategy, Unintended effect prevention
- **Epistemic Stance:** Pragmatic-Systemic
- **Learnability:** Conceptual + practice — improves with deliberate "and then what?" discipline
- **Complements:** Systems Thinking, Inversion, Probabilistic Thinking, Pre-mortem
- **Anti-patterns:** Infinite regress; paralysis from seeing too many consequences
