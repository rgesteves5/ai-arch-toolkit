# 07 — Integration: Rules, Anti-Patterns & Maturity Model

Principles for combining systems fluidly, common mistakes, and the developmental path from beginner to expert.

---

## 10 Integration Flow Rules

### Rule 1: Classify Before You Choose
Always start with Cynefin or Sensemaking. The number one failure is using the wrong approach for the situation. An expert-analysis approach in a complex domain wastes time. An experimental approach in a clear domain wastes effort. Classify first, then select tools.

### Rule 2: Diverge Before You Converge
In any creative or problem-solving process, always explore broadly before evaluating narrowly. Divergent Thinking, Lateral Thinking, First Principles, and Munger's Latticework come BEFORE Critical Thinking, Convergent Thinking, and Bayesian evaluation. Premature convergence is the most common creative failure.

### Rule 3: Metacognition Runs Always
Metacognition is not a step — it's a background process. At every phase, monitor: Is my current approach working? Am I confused? Am I overconfident? Should I switch tools? The moment metacognition flags a problem, pause and reassess.

### Rule 4: Inversion Bookends Everything
Use Inversion at the start ("what would make this problem worse?") to understand the problem, AND at the end ("how could this solution fail?") to stress-test the solution. Inversion is the cheapest, fastest quality check available.

### Rule 5: Match Locus to Situation
Individual tools (First Principles, Bayesian Thinking) work for solo decisions. Dialogical tools (Socratic Method, Six Hats) work for group exploration. Team tools (Design Thinking, Pre-mortem) work for collaborative projects. Using a team tool solo (or vice versa) reduces effectiveness.

### Rule 6: Cycle, Don't Waterfall
The Master Sequence is a cycle, not a one-pass waterfall. You will loop back. Testing reveals you framed the wrong problem → return to Phase 2. Action generates new observations → return to Phase 1. The OODA Loop's central insight is that the cycle never ends.

### Rule 7: Depth Is Proportional to Stakes
Not every decision needs 8 thinking systems. Use Bounded Rationality as a governor: for low-stakes decisions, a quick OODA cycle with satisficing is sufficient. For career-defining, bet-the-company, or life-altering decisions, deploy the full stack.

### Rule 8: Body First in Ambiguity
When you're stuck, confused, or overwhelmed, don't try harder with analytical tools. Switch to Embodied Cognition: walk, sketch, build, change your environment. Switch to Sensemaking: tell someone the story of what's happening. These approaches generate insights that pure analysis cannot.

### Rule 9: Update, Don't Anchor
Every piece of new information is an opportunity for Bayesian updating. Don't fall in love with your first framing, your first solution, or your first mental model. The OODA Loop's core insight is that the person who can destroy and rebuild mental models fastest wins. Treat all conclusions as provisional.

### Rule 10: The Latticework Is the Long Game
Munger's Latticework is not a tool you use in a moment — it's a tool you build over a lifetime. Every time you learn a new system, you add a node to your latticework. Every time you use one, you strengthen the connections. The goal isn't to memorize 24 systems — it's to build an intuitive sense of which combination fits the situation.

## 8 Integration Anti-Patterns

### ❌ Tool Fetishism
**Symptom:** Applying your favorite system to every situation regardless of fit.
**Example:** Using First Principles thinking to decide what to have for lunch. Using Design Thinking for a problem with a known solution.
**Fix:** Always start with Cynefin. Match the tool to the domain, not your preference.

### ❌ Premature Convergence
**Symptom:** Jumping to Critical Thinking and evaluation before generating enough options.
**Example:** Brainstorming for 5 minutes, then spending 2 hours evaluating 3 mediocre ideas.
**Fix:** Enforce a divergence quota. 20+ ideas minimum before any evaluation begins.

### ❌ Analysis Paralysis via Systems Thinking
**Symptom:** "Everything is connected" becomes an excuse to never act because the full picture isn't clear.
**Example:** Mapping feedback loops for months without ever running an experiment.
**Fix:** Pair Systems Thinking with Theory of Constraints (find ONE leverage point) and Bounded Rationality (act on "good enough" understanding).

### ❌ Speed Without Orientation (Misused OODA)
**Symptom:** Cycling through OODA rapidly but with poor orientation — making fast, wrong decisions.
**Example:** Launching product updates every week without understanding why the last ones failed.
**Fix:** Invest disproportionately in the Orient phase. Quality of sensemaking > speed of action.

### ❌ Meta-Cognitive Paralysis
**Symptom:** Thinking about thinking about thinking — infinite recursion that prevents actual thought.
**Example:** Spending 30 minutes deciding which thinking framework to use for a 10-minute problem.
**Fix:** Use Bounded Rationality as a governor. For most problems, your first instinct about which tool to use is fine. Reserve meta-analysis for high-stakes situations.

### ❌ Solo Tools for Group Problems (and vice versa)
**Symptom:** Trying to do Socratic questioning on yourself (becomes self-reinforcing), or using Six Thinking Hats alone (misses diverse perspectives).
**Fix:** Match the locus tag. Individual tools for individual thinking. Dialogical tools require actual dialogue.

### ❌ Collecting Without Connecting
**Symptom:** Learning many frameworks but never building the latticework — each system exists as an isolated concept.
**Example:** Knowing about Bayesian thinking AND First Principles but never combining them.
**Fix:** After learning each system, explicitly connect it to 3+ others. Practice combinations on real problems.

### ❌ Skipping Phase 0 (Orientation)
**Symptom:** Diving into problem-solving without first classifying the situation.
**Example:** Applying complicated-domain methods (expert analysis, root cause) to complex-domain problems (emergence, experimentation needed).
**Fix:** ALWAYS ask "what domain am I in?" first. 60 seconds of Cynefin classification prevents hours of wasted effort.

## The Integration Maturity Model

### Level 1 — Unconscious Incompetence
*"I don't know what I don't know."*

You use whatever thinking habit you already have for every situation. You reason by analogy, defer to authority, or go with gut feel. You don't realize there are other ways to think.

**Learn first:** Dual-Process Theory (awareness of your own cognitive autopilot) and Metacognition (begin monitoring your own thinking).

### Level 2 — Single Tool
*"I have a hammer."*

You've learned one thinking system well — maybe Critical Thinking, maybe Systems Thinking — and you apply it everywhere. It works better than Level 1, but you force-fit problems to your tool.

**Learn next:** Cynefin (realize different problems need different approaches) and Munger's Latticework (realize you need multiple models).

### Level 3 — Multiple Tools, Sequential
*"I have a toolbox."*

You know several systems and can apply them one at a time. You might use Design Thinking for one project and First Principles for another. But you don't combine them within a single problem or switch fluidly between them.

**Learn next:** Convergent/Divergent Thinking (learn to sequence tools within a problem) and OODA Loop (learn to cycle continuously).

### Level 4 — Conscious Combination
*"I can orchestrate."*

You deliberately combine systems: Cynefin to classify → appropriate tools to execute → Metacognition to monitor → Bayesian updating as results come in. You follow playbooks like those in Document 06. It's effortful but effective.

**Deepen:** Sensemaking, Dialectical Thinking, Second-Order Thinking (the subtler, harder systems that distinguish good thinkers from great ones).

### Level 5 — Fluid Integration
*"The tools disappear."*

You no longer consciously select systems. Your Kernel layer (Metacognition, Dual-Process awareness, Embodied Cognition) operates automatically. You flow between divergent and convergent, between analysis and intuition, between individual and distributed cognition — guided by the situation, not by deliberate framework selection. This is Munger's latticework fully internalized.

This level takes years of deliberate practice.

## Recommended Learning Sequence

| Stage | Systems to Learn | Why This Order |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|
| 1 | Metacognition + Dual-Process Theory | Understand your own mind first |
| 2 | Cynefin + Critical Thinking | Classify situations + evaluate rigorously |
| 3 | Divergent/Convergent + Socratic Method | Creative and questioning skills |
| 4 | Systems Thinking + First Principles | Deep structural understanding |
| 5 | OODA Loop + Bayesian Thinking | Dynamic decision-making |
| 6 | Inversion + Pre-mortem + Second-Order Thinking | Strategic stress-testing |
| 7 | Design Thinking + Sensemaking + Dialectical Thinking | Human and complex systems |
| 8 | Munger's Latticework + Distributed/Embodied Cognition | The integration layer |

Each stage builds on the previous. Don't rush. Depth in a few systems beats shallow familiarity with all of them.
