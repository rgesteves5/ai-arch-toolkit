# 03 — Categorization Axes

Five orthogonal ways to categorize all 24 thinking systems, showing that no single dimension captures the full picture.

---

## Axis 1: Descriptive vs. Prescriptive

*Does it explain how we actually think, or tell us how we should think?*

### Descriptive (models of cognition)
Dual-Process Theory, Bounded Rationality, Embodied Cognition, Distributed Cognition, Convergent/Divergent Thinking

### Prescriptive (methods to think better)
Socratic Method, Inversion, Pre-mortem, OODA Loop, Design Thinking, First Principles, Lateral Thinking, Theory of Constraints, Probabilistic Thinking, Second-Order Thinking

### Both
Bayesian Thinking, Metacognition, Systems Thinking, Sensemaking, Dialectical Thinking, Deductive/Inductive/Abductive Reasoning, Cynefin Framework

### Meta-framework
Munger's Latticework (a framework for combining frameworks)

---

## Axis 2: Phase of the Thinking Process

Every problem moves through stages. Different systems serve different stages.

### Phase 0 — Orienting ("What kind of situation is this?")
Cynefin Framework, Sensemaking, Metacognition

### Phase 1 — Perceiving ("What's actually happening?")
OODA Loop (Observe), Systems Thinking, Embodied Cognition, Distributed Cognition, Sensemaking

### Phase 2 — Framing ("What's the real problem?")
Design Thinking (Empathize/Define), First Principles, Socratic Method, Dialectical Thinking, Inversion, Cynefin Framework, Systems Thinking, Theory of Constraints

### Phase 3 — Generating ("What could we do?")
Divergent Thinking, Lateral Thinking, First Principles, Dialectical Thinking, Munger's Latticework, Design Thinking (Ideate)

### Phase 4 — Evaluating ("What should we do?")
Convergent Thinking, Critical Thinking, Bayesian Thinking, Second-Order Thinking, Probabilistic Thinking, Pre-mortem, Inversion, Deductive/Inductive/Abductive

### Phase 5 — Deciding & Acting ("Commit and execute.")
OODA Loop (Decide/Act), Bounded Rationality, Theory of Constraints

### Phase 6 — Reflecting & Learning ("What did we learn?")
Metacognition, Sensemaking (retrospective), Bayesian updating, Critical Thinking

---

## Axis 3: Where Does the Thinking Live?

### Inside one mind (Individual)
Metacognition, Dual-Process Theory, Bayesian Thinking, First Principles, Inversion, Probabilistic Thinking, Second-Order Thinking, Bounded Rationality, Munger's Latticework

### Between minds (Dialogical — requires conversation)
Socratic Method, Six Thinking Hats, Dialectical Thinking

### Across a team (Team — collaborative process)
Design Thinking, Pre-mortem, Theory of Constraints, Cynefin Framework, Sensemaking

### Across minds, tools, and environments (Distributed)
Distributed Cognition, Systems Thinking, OODA Loop

### In the body and physical environment
Embodied Cognition

---

## Axis 4: What Kind of Problem Does It Fit?

*(Borrowing Cynefin's own domain classification)*

### Clear (known solution exists)
Critical Thinking, Deductive Reasoning, Bloom's Taxonomy

### Complicated (experts can solve it)
Systems Thinking, Theory of Constraints, Convergent Thinking, Second-Order Thinking, Bayesian Thinking

### Complex (emergent, unpredictable)
Sensemaking, Design Thinking, Dialectical Thinking, Probabilistic Thinking, Divergent Thinking, Cynefin Framework

### Chaotic (act first)
OODA Loop, Bounded Rationality (satisfice now), Sensemaking

### Novel (no precedent)
First Principles, Lateral Thinking, Inversion

### Competitive (adversarial)
OODA Loop, Second-Order Thinking, Inversion, Probabilistic Thinking

### Meta (classifies problem types)
Cynefin Framework

### All problem types
Metacognition, Dual-Process Theory, Munger's Latticework, Convergent/Divergent Thinking

---

## Axis 5: Temporal Orientation

### Backward-looking (learning from what happened)
Sensemaking (retrospective), Metacognition (reflection), Bayesian updating, Critical Thinking (evidence evaluation)

### Present-focused (making sense of now)
OODA Loop (Observe/Orient), Cynefin Framework, Embodied Cognition, Dual-Process Theory, Distributed Cognition, Sensemaking

### Forward-looking (anticipating what's next)
Pre-mortem, Second-Order Thinking, Probabilistic Thinking, Systems Thinking (leverage points), Inversion, Theory of Constraints

---

## Why Multi-Dimensional Classification Matters

No single axis captures a thinking system's identity. For example:

- **Bayesian Thinking** is descriptive AND prescriptive, serves the evaluating phase, is individual, works for complicated AND complex problems, and is both present and backward-looking.
- **The Socratic Method** is prescriptive, serves framing/evaluating/reflecting, is dialogical (requires another person), works for all problem types, and is present-focused.

The most useful approach is a **tag-based system** where each thinking system gets tagged on all five axes (plus the 7 additional dimensions from the full taxonomy), allowing you to query from any direction: "I'm in a complex situation, at the framing stage, working alone, looking forward — what should I use?"
