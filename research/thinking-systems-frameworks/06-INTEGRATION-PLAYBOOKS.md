# 06 — Integration: Situation-Based Playbooks

Pre-built combinations for 8 common real-world scenarios, with step-by-step tool sequences.

---

## Playbook 1: Crisis Response — "I need to act NOW"

**Domain:** Chaotic → Act first, sense second

1. **OODA Loop** — Observe what you can in seconds, orient based on experience, decide, ACT
2. **Bounded Rationality** — Satisfice. Don't optimize. The first workable option is the right one
3. **Sensemaking** — After acting, construct a narrative: what just happened? What worked?
4. **Metacognition** — Am I in fight-or-flight? Force System 2 engagement for the next decision
5. **Cynefin** — Has the situation moved from Chaotic to Complex? If so, switch approaches

---

## Playbook 2: Breakthrough Innovation — "Conventional approaches have failed"

**Domain:** Novel/Complex → Break patterns, build from scratch

1. **Socratic Method** — Challenge every assumption. "Why do we do it this way?"
2. **First Principles** — Decompose to fundamental truths. Discard conventions
3. **Lateral Thinking** — Use random entry, provocation, and challenge to generate wild ideas
4. **Divergent Thinking** — 50 ideas minimum. No judgment. Quantity is the goal
5. **Munger's Latticework** — View the problem through biology, physics, psychology, economics
6. **Dialectical Thinking** — Can opposing approaches be synthesized?
7. **Convergent Thinking** — Now evaluate: which ideas survive rigorous testing?
8. **Pre-mortem** — Imagine the best idea failed. Why?

---

## Playbook 3: High-Stakes Decision Under Uncertainty

**Domain:** Complicated/Complex → Analyze deeply, but don't chase certainty

1. **Cynefin** — Is this complicated (experts can solve it) or complex (need to experiment)?
2. **Systems Thinking** — Map the key variables, feedback loops, and stakeholders
3. **Bayesian Thinking** — What are my priors? What evidence would change my mind?
4. **Second-Order Thinking** — "And then what?" three times minimum
5. **Inversion** — How could this decision destroy value? What am I not seeing?
6. **Pre-mortem** — Team exercise: "We chose this and it failed. Why?"
7. **Probabilistic Thinking** — Assign probabilities. Calculate expected values
8. **Bounded Rationality** — At some point, decide. Perfect information doesn't exist
9. **OODA Loop** — Act, observe the results, orient, and decide again

---

## Playbook 4: Understanding a Complex System

**Domain:** Complex → Map, model, find leverage

1. **Systems Thinking** — Draw causal loop diagrams. Identify stocks, flows, feedback loops
2. **Distributed Cognition** — Who else holds knowledge? What tools capture information?
3. **Cynefin** — Which parts of this system are clear, complicated, or complex?
4. **Sensemaking** — What narrative have people been telling about this system? Is it accurate?
5. **Socratic Method** — Question the dominant narrative. What's being overlooked?
6. **Theory of Constraints** — Where's the bottleneck? What limits the system's performance?
7. **Second-Order Thinking** — If we intervene here, what ripple effects follow?
8. **Dialectical Thinking** — What tensions in the system are actually productive?

---

## Playbook 5: Human-Centered Problem Solving

**Domain:** Complex (human) → Empathize, prototype, iterate

1. **Design Thinking (Empathize)** — Observe, interview, immerse. Don't assume you know the need
2. **Embodied Cognition** — Be physically present in the user's context. Feel it
3. **Socratic Method** — Ask the users deep questions. Challenge your assumptions about them
4. **Design Thinking (Define)** — Reframe the problem based on what you learned
5. **Divergent Thinking** — Generate many possible solutions
6. **Lateral Thinking** — Add creative provocation to break past obvious solutions
7. **Design Thinking (Prototype)** — Build quick, cheap, tangible versions
8. **Design Thinking (Test)** — Put prototypes in front of real users
9. **Bayesian Thinking** — Update your understanding based on test results
10. **Iterate** — Return to any earlier step based on what you learned

## Playbook 6: Competitive Strategy — "I need to win"

**Domain:** Competitive/Dynamic → Speed + quality of orientation

1. **OODA Loop** — Observe broadly. What is the opponent doing? What's the environment?
2. **OODA (Orient)** — Build/destroy/rebuild mental models rapidly. This is the key battleground
3. **Munger's Latticework** — Apply models from game theory, psychology, military strategy
4. **Second-Order Thinking** — If I do X, they'll do Y. Then what?
5. **Inversion** — What would guarantee I lose? Avoid those things
6. **Probabilistic Thinking** — What are the odds of each scenario?
7. **OODA (Decide + Act)** — Move faster than the opponent's cycle. Disrupt their orientation
8. **Sensemaking** — After action: what happened? Why? Feed into next OODA cycle

---

## Playbook 7: Deep Learning — "I need to master a new domain"

**Domain:** Personal development → Meta-cognitive mastery

1. **Metacognition** — What do I already know? What don't I know? What's my learning strategy?
2. **Socratic Method** — Question my existing beliefs about the topic
3. **First Principles** — What are the fundamental building blocks of this domain?
4. **Munger's Latticework** — How does this connect to what I know from other fields?
5. **Divergent Thinking** — Explore broadly before narrowing
6. **Bayesian Thinking** — Update my understanding with each new piece of evidence
7. **Dialectical Thinking** — What contradictions or tensions exist in this field?
8. **Embodied Cognition** — Teach it, build it, practice it physically — not just read about it
9. **Metacognition** — Reassess: How has my understanding changed? What gaps remain?

---

## Playbook 8: Group Facilitation — "I need collective intelligence"

**Domain:** Social/Dialogical → Harness distributed thinking

1. **Distributed Cognition** — Who knows what? How is expertise distributed?
2. **Six Thinking Hats** — Structure the discussion: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, ideas, process
3. **Socratic Method** — Ask questions that draw out everyone's reasoning
4. **Dialectical Thinking** — Surface tensions. Frame them as "both/and" not "either/or"
5. **Sensemaking** — Build a shared narrative that integrates diverse perspectives
6. **Pre-mortem** — "We went with this approach and it failed. Why?" (gives permission to voice concerns)
7. **Convergent Thinking** — Evaluate options against shared criteria
8. **Metacognition (group)** — "How are we thinking? Are we falling into groupthink?"
